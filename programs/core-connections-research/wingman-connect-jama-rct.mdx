## Key Points

**Question:** Does group training to build cohesion, shared purpose, and healthy coping for classes of new US Air Force Airmen reduce suicidal thoughts, depression symptoms, and occupational problems?

**Findings:** In this cluster RCT of 1,485 personnel in 215 training classes, Wingman-Connect reduced suicidal ideation, depression symptoms, and occupational problems at 1 month by fostering cohesive, healthy classes. Reduced depression symptoms were maintained through 6 months (OR 0.80 for elevated depression at either follow-up).

**Meaning:** Wingman-Connect is the first universal prevention program to reduce suicidal ideation and depression in a general Air Force population.

## Abstract

**Design:** Cluster RCT, October 2017–October 2019, at a US Air Force technical training school; 216 classes randomized, 84% retention.

**Interventions:** Wingman-Connect used group skill building for cohesion, shared purpose, and managing career/personal stressors (3 blocks of 2 hours). Stress management (active control) covered cognitive/behavioral strategies (2 hours). Both had a 1-hour booster plus text messages.

**Main Outcomes:** Suicidal ideation and depression scales (Computerized Adaptive Test for Mental Health) and self-reported occupational impairment. Hypothesized mediators: class cohesion, morale, healthy norms, and bonds to classmates.

**Results:** 1,485 individuals (1,222 men; mean age 20.9). At **1 month**, Wingman-Connect showed lower suicidal ideation (ES −0.23, _P_ = .001), lower depression (ES −0.24, _P_ = .002), and fewer occupational problems (ES −0.14, _P_ = .02). At **6 months**, depression remained lower (ES −0.16, _P_ = .03); suicidal ideation was directionally lower but not significant (ES −0.13, _P_ = .06). Number needed to treat for 1 fewer elevated-depression case: **21**. The program strengthened cohesive, healthy class units, which mediated reductions in suicidal ideation (_P_ = .02) and depression (_P_ = .02) at 1 month.

**Conclusion:** Wingman-Connect is the first universal prevention program shown to reduce suicidal ideation and depression in a general Air Force population.

## Background

Active duty military suicide rates (24.8/100,000 in 2018) now equal the general population's, after decades of being lower. Existing prevention focuses on identifying and treating those already suicidal or high-risk — necessary but insufficient on its own. No universal prevention program had previously been shown to reduce suicidal thoughts/behaviors in military populations; recent coping/interpersonal skill trials in Canadian and Royal Air Force recruits found no mental-health benefit, suggesting cognitive training alone lacks ecological validity without addressing unit functioning.

Wingman-Connect is a **network health** intervention: it targets natural organizational units to strengthen bonds, cohesion, and adaptive coping norms. Disrupted relationships are the most common psychosocial stressor preceding military suicides, and unit cohesion is associated with lower suicidal ideation; Army STARRS data show up to 20% of suicide attempts over 5 years were associated with unit membership where another member had attempted suicide.

## Methods Summary

- **Setting:** Technical Training School, Sheppard Air Force Base (365 Avionics, 363 Weapons Systems squadrons).
- **Design:** Matched-pair cluster randomization (job, class duration, size, start date); 107 classes (748 Airmen) to Wingman-Connect, 108 classes (737 Airmen) to stress management.
- **Wingman-Connect:** 3 sessions of 90–120 min over 3 consecutive days, built around **Kinship, Purpose, Guidance, Balance**; peer-nominated "training champions"; 1-hour booster at 1 month; training fidelity 97.7%.
- **Stress management (active control):** 2-hour CBT-based session on the stress response system and coping strategies.
- **Analysis:** Intention-to-treat, multilevel mixed-effects models with random intercept for class, adjusted for baseline outcome, sex, age, race/ethnicity, active-duty status. Two-level mediation models tested the network health pathway.

## Results

### Table — Primary Outcomes (Effect Size, 95% CI)

| Outcome | 1-Month ES | _P_ | 6-Month ES | _P_ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Suicidal ideation severity | −0.23 (−0.39, −0.09) | .001 | −0.13 (−0.29, 0.01) | .06 |
| Depression symptoms | −0.24 (−0.41, −0.08) | .002 | −0.16 (−0.34, −0.02) | .03 |
| Occupational impairment | −0.14 (−0.31, −0.02) | .02 | 0.01 (−0.12, 0.11) | .82 |

Wingman-Connect participants were also less likely to receive corrective training (OR 0.51) or a negative counseling statement (OR 0.50) at 1 month. Odds of elevated depression at either follow-up were 20% lower (OR 0.80, NNT = 21); elevated suicidal ideation was 19% lower but not significant (OR 0.81, NNT = 44).

### Mediators

Wingman-Connect increased class cohesion, morale, healthy norms, and bonds to classmates (ES 0.18–0.23), positive career behaviors (ES 0.16), and reduced anxiety (ES −0.14) and anger (ES −0.18). A latent "cohesive healthy class networks" factor significantly mediated the intervention's effect on both suicidal ideation and depression at 1 month — confirming the underlying network health model.

## Discussion

The 1-month effect sizes (−0.23 to −0.24) matched state-of-the-art universal prevention programs for broad, mostly non-high-risk populations. Benefits were distributed across baseline severity levels, sex, and age — a strength of universal prevention for populations with members who may not seek mental health services. The dual benefit for occupational functioning and mental health (fewer disciplinary actions) illustrates why upstream, network-based prevention can be more sustainable than clinical-only approaches: it strengthens job-relevant skills at the same time it reduces suicide risk.

### Limitations

Participants and leadership were not blinded to condition; outcomes relied on self-report (though validated against clinical interviews); trainers were research staff rather than military personnel, which may limit generalizability.

## Conclusion

Wingman-Connect is the first universal prevention program shown in a randomized trial to reduce suicidal ideation and depression symptoms in a general, non-clinical Air Force population, with benefits on depression sustained at 6 months. Extension to the operational Air Force is recommended to test longer-term impact on suicidal behavior.
