This brief outlines a new approach to military suicide prevention that addresses a critical gap: reducing suicide risk among service members whose suicide concerns are not disclosed or otherwise identified. It focuses on the strategy and evidence behind the Connect Program's peer network-based approach.

## The Critical Gap

Most military suicide deaths occur among personnel who are not identified as high-risk and are not receiving mental health care. In the Active Component in 2023:

- **58%** had no behavioral health diagnosis
- **Two-thirds** received no mental health services in the 90 days before death

These "hidden suicidal members" remain outside traditional prevention efforts despite having active suicidal thoughts, significant emotional difficulties, social isolation, and unmet prevention needs.

## Limitations of Current Prevention Strategies

Traditional military suicide prevention centers on encouraging help-seeking, reducing stigma, improving treatment effectiveness, teaching individual coping skills, and self-help resources. These approaches save lives and are essential to continue — but they cannot reach members who won't seek help, don't show recognizable warning signs, or don't match traditional screening criteria. A new approach is needed to reduce suicide risk **without requiring identification of individual members at high risk**.

## Connect Program: A New Approach

The Connect Program strengthens the natural peer networks that protect and support all unit members. Developed as **Wingman-Connect** through a 10-year University of Rochester / Department of the Air Force collaboration (DoD funded 2014–2023), it delivers six hours of interactive group training plus six months of text/video reinforcement, built around four core strengths: **Kinship, Purpose, Guidance, and Balance**.

## Key Evidence: Direct Impact on Existing Risk

A gold-standard RCT with 1,500 Air Force early-career personnel demonstrated Wingman-Connect significantly reduced suicide risk, depressive symptoms, and occupational problems (Wyman et al., 2020) — with **50% fewer** incidents requiring corrective training.

A follow-up network analysis (Wyman et al., 2022) showed _how_ it works:

- Members with elevated suicide risk in **untrained** units showed **increasing isolation** from peers over time
- The same high-risk-profile members in Wingman-Connect units **increased** their healthy peer connections
- This prevention of isolation occurred **without identifying which members were at risk**
- The program's positive effects were strongest among those who needed it most

## Why the Peer Group Approach Works

1. **Participant role is acceptable and meaningful** — members participate as teammates, not patients, within normal duty settings.
2. **Strengthens the group to strengthen the individual** — builds protective peer networks, reduces isolation naturally without singling anyone out.
3. **Prevention through mission enhancement** — integrates prevention directly into unit functioning and readiness.

## Reducing Critical Barriers

- **Reaches hidden risk** — works without requiring self-identification.
- **Eliminates access barriers** — delivered in standard training settings, no referral needed, reduces occupational problems by 50%.
- **Sustainable** — integrates with existing military structures and improves both prevention and readiness.

## Program Adaptability

Ongoing projects with law enforcement and civilian settings suggest the Connect Program adapts successfully to other high-stress environments, with strong engagement and acceptability demonstrated in civilian law enforcement pilots (Pisani et al., under review).

## Conclusion

The Connect Program gives military leaders a way to reduce suicide deaths by directly addressing risk among members who won't be identified through traditional means, creating protective environments for all members with the strongest effects for those at highest risk, and complementing existing clinical services with a population-level approach.
