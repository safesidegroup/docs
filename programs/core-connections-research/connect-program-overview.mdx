## Strength in the Unit, Not Just the Individual

Every leader knows: when the team is strong, the mission succeeds. When it's fractured, the cracks show at the worst possible moment. Connect isn't just about individual coping — it's about strengthening the bonds and health of the network, so no one falls through the cracks.

## Strategic Overview

The Connect Program is a scientifically validated initiative to enhance unit cohesion, force readiness, suicide prevention, and peer support. Developed through a 10-year University of Rochester / Department of the Air Force collaboration (DoD funded 2014–2023), Connect is now the official resiliency program of the Air Force, currently undergoing rapid, deliberate expansion.

A gold-standard RCT with 1,500 Air Force personnel (Wyman et al., _JAMA Network Open_, 2020) validated the program:

- Suicide risk dropped significantly in Connect-trained groups.
- Depressive symptoms decreased and remained lower for six months.
- Occupational performance improved, with a **50% reduction** in corrective training instances.

Strategically, Connect supports force preservation: fewer operational disruptions from preventable personnel losses, decreased disciplinary actions, strengthened small-unit cohesion, and an efficient 8-hour delivery format.

## Prevention Approach and Features

Connect represents a fundamental shift from traditional suicide prevention training long identified as ineffective and unpopular with service members (SPRIRC, 2022). Delivered over three sessions (6–8 hours total), it builds unit-wide protective factors through peer-to-peer learning rather than lecture-based "death by PowerPoint."

The program is structured around four research-validated strengths — the **Four Cores**:

- **Kinship** — strengthening bonds within teams to foster trust, mutual support, and shared responsibility.
- **Purpose** — reinforcing individual and collective commitment to mission readiness and well-being.
- **Guidance** — equipping personnel to recognize challenges, seek support, and direct struggling peers.
- **Balance** — proactive management of stress, workload, and personal challenges.

### Skills Framework: Mission-Ready Training

> "What we have learned and relearned in our Army is that unit cohesion and teamwork are what give individual soldiers the confidence to use initiative, to be resourceful, and to be all they can be." — Sergeant Major Glen E. Morrell, US Army

Connect uses a social learning and network enhancement model (Bandura, 1977; Wyman et al., 2019, 2020, 2022): skills are acquired through modeling, peer interaction, and group norm development rather than passive instruction — developed both individually and for the group, and reinforced through natural daily unit activities beyond the initial training.

## Addressing Hidden Risk Without Singling People Out

Most service members who die by suicide are never formally identified as high-risk (58% had no behavioral health diagnosis; two-thirds received no mental health services in the 90 days before death). Connect directly reduces isolation and strengthens unit cohesion without requiring at-risk individuals to self-identify:

- In untrained units, at-risk individuals tend to become more isolated over time.
- In Connect-trained units, at-risk individuals increase peer connections and support, naturally integrating into the protective network.
- Thriving individuals lift up and strengthen the group, including more vulnerable members.

## Research and Validation

The gold-standard RCT with 1,500 Air Force personnel found suicide risk dropped significantly, depressive symptoms decreased and stayed lower for six months, and occupational performance improved with 50% fewer corrective training instances. The active mechanism was formation of more cohesive groups unified around practices supporting higher morale and mission readiness (Wyman et al., 2020).

## Conclusion

Connect strengthens mission readiness by fostering unit-wide cohesion, reduces suicide risk without requiring at-risk identification, and reinforces military values by positioning prevention within operational effectiveness — a battle-tested, research-backed, operationally efficient approach to protecting warfighter well-being.
