CAMS — the Collaborative Assessment and Management of Suicidality — is an evidence-based, suicide-specific treatment developed by Dr. David Jobes and backed by more than 35 years of rigorous clinical research. Unlike general mental health interventions, CAMS directly and collaboratively addresses suicide and its drivers, working with patients rather than simply treating them. It is proven to reduce suicidal ideation in as few as six sessions with a trained therapist, making it one of the most efficient and impactful approaches available to clinicians today. SafeSide Prevention offers CAMS training to help licensed clinical professionals integrate this framework into their day-to-day practice quickly and confidently.

## What Is Included in CAMS Training

Your CAMS training experience includes four integrated components designed to build knowledge, develop practical skills, and embed the framework into real clinical work.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Foundational Video Course">
    Engage with foundational CAMS knowledge through structured video instruction, including a 12-session clinical demonstration delivered by CAMS founder Dr. David Jobes. This immersive course gives you direct insight into how CAMS unfolds across the arc of treatment — from the first collaborative assessment to discharge planning.
  </Step>
  <Step title="CAMS Sourcebook: Managing Suicidal Risk, 3rd Ed.">
    You receive a copy of _Managing Suicidal Risk: A Collaborative Approach_ — the definitive sourcebook for the CAMS Framework® and an essential reference for applying the framework in your own clinical work with patients across the lifespan.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Online Role-Play Training Day">
    Practice key components of the CAMS Framework® — including completing and reviewing the Suicide Status Form — in a structured, supportive online environment. This role-play training day bridges the gap between conceptual learning and real clinical application.
  </Step>
  <Step title="4 CAMS Consultation Calls">
    Participate in four live online consultation calls with experienced CAMS consultants. These sessions provide personalized guidance, case discussion, shared best practices, and ongoing support as you integrate CAMS into your practice.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  CAMS certification is achieved with **10 hours of online coursework** combined with **4 consultation calls**, designed for comprehensive learning without disrupting your existing clinical caseload.
</Note>

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Who can become CAMS Trained™?">
    The CAMS training program is intended for licensed clinical professionals who wish to enhance their competency in managing and treating suicidal risk among patients. This includes psychologists, social workers, counselors, psychiatrists, and other licensed mental health clinicians.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How long does CAMS certification take?">
    CAMS certification can be achieved with 10 hours of online coursework and 4 consultation calls. The self-paced nature of the video course means you can complete it on your own schedule, fitting training around your existing clinical commitments.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does the CAMS Framework® cover both adolescents and adults?">
    Yes. The CAMS Framework® is backed by over 35 years of ongoing clinical research demonstrating its effectiveness in significantly reducing suicidal ideation and overall patient distress for both adolescents and adults. A developmentally informed adaptation, CAMS-4Teens, is also available for clinicians working specifically with young people.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What makes CAMS different from other suicide prevention programs?">
    CAMS stands out due to its evidence-based, collaborative approach that places the patient as an active partner in their own treatment. Unlike programs focused on risk stratification or general mental health, CAMS directly targets suicide and its underlying drivers. It has demonstrated proven results in reducing suicidal ideation in as few as six sessions, while also enhancing hope, treatment acceptability, and clinical retention.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
