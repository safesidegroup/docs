SafeSide Restore provides leaders with a trusted network and a suite of educational resources to help their organization build a Restorative, Just, and Learning Culture (RJLC) in response to suicide-related incidents. Drawing on the influential work of Turner et al. (2020, 2022), published in the *Australian and New Zealand Journal of Psychiatry*, Restore moves organizations away from blame-focused retrospective reviews toward a process that engages the people most impacted, identifies human hurts and needs, and provides a genuine path toward healing, learning, and systemic improvement. Through bi-monthly working meetings with peers and subject matter experts, leaders share progress, unblock challenges, and plan next steps together.

## Who Benefits from SafeSide Restore

Leaders and organizations across diverse sectors can find value in SafeSide Restore. It is particularly beneficial for leaders in:

- **Primary Health** — general practice, community health, and allied health services
- **Mental Health** — inpatient, community, and private mental health settings
- **Youth Services** — organizations working with children and young people
- **Alcohol and Other Drugs Services** — counseling and treatment services
- **Police** — law enforcement organizations responding to suicide incidents
- **Military** — defence forces navigating service member incidents

## Why Reframe Your Approach to Incidents

Most organizations default to asking "what went wrong?" after a suicide-related incident. SafeSide Restore helps you shift the fundamental question to "who has been hurt, and what do they need?"

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Improved Support for Families" icon="house-heart">
    Families often feel alienated from traditional incident review processes, leading to further hurt and a desire to seek external investigations. A Restorative Just and Learning Culture process provides meaningful support to families and generates learning that improves future care.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Staff Retention After Incidents" icon="user-check">
    Organizations lose valuable staff members after critical incidents when proper support is not provided. This is especially costly given existing workforce challenges in health sectors. A restorative approach addresses the burnout and disillusionment that otherwise drives experienced clinicians to leave.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Breaking the Cycle of Ineffective Recommendations" icon="rotate">
    Poor stakeholder engagement leads to weak recommendations that no one implements. This creates a recurring cycle where the same problems return because previous recommendations were not effective or followed through. Better engagement, grounded in the experiences of those affected, leads to better recommendations and genuine implementation.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Evidence Informing SafeSide Restore

SafeSide Restore's approach is grounded in peer-reviewed research and developed in partnership with international leaders in restorative practice within healthcare.

<Note>
  The foundational evidence base includes **Turner et al. (2020)** — *"Addressing Inconvenient Truths in Suicide Prevention: A Call for a Restorative Just Culture"* — and **Turner et al. (2022)** — *"Restorative Just Culture Outcomes"* — both published in the *Australian and New Zealand Journal of Psychiatry*. These studies examine significant advances in healthcare incident response through the implementation of Restorative Just Culture combined with Safety II principles.
</Note>
