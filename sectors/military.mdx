## Core Connections: The Evidence

Core Connections is the only upstream military prevention program proven effective in a **gold-standard randomized controlled trial (RCT) with 1,500 Airmen** (Wyman et al., 2020, 2022), published in JAMA Network Open. The study demonstrated significant reductions in psychiatric diagnoses and medically documented suicidal behavior among new members of the US Air Force.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="50% Less Corrective Training" icon="award">
    Participants are **50% less likely** to need corrective training or receive a negative counseling statement, with fewer relationship problems due to anger and disconnection.
  </Card>
  <Card title="5× Less Isolated" icon="link">
    After Core Connections, service members who previously considered suicide were **5 times less likely** to feel isolated from their unit.
  </Card>
  <Card title="3× More Positive Connections" icon="handshake">
    Participants at elevated risk for suicide were **3 times more likely** to report positive social connections within their unit following the program.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  Core Connections reduces hidden suicide risk precisely because it doesn't rely on at-risk individuals identifying themselves or on team leaders detecting individual warning signs. Instead, it strengthens the social network of the whole unit — so vulnerable service members are naturally drawn closer, not left on the periphery.
</Note>

## SafeSide CARE: Scale and Reach

SafeSide CARE's reach in military and defence contexts demonstrates its suitability for large-scale deployment.

**67,300 Australian Defence Force personnel** completed the Defence Suicide Awareness training between 1 January 2025 and 30 June 2025 — a program developed through the Defence SafeSide Project. The project was recognized with the **Best Practice in Workplace Award** by Suicide Prevention Australia.

CARE evaluations show that confidence in responding to suicide concerns rose from **72% to 95%** following the workshop — giving commanders and supervisors a concrete measure of workforce capability.

  <Accordion title="How does SafeSide address hidden suicide risk?">
    Most military prevention programs rely on at-risk individuals self-identifying, or on leaders detecting warning signs in specific individuals. Core Connections takes a different approach: it strengthens the social network of the entire unit, so that service members who are at risk but not visibly struggling are naturally kept closer to their peers rather than drifting into isolation.

    SafeSide CARE complements this by equipping commanders and supervisors with a clear, consistent framework for responding when they do observe distress in a team member — closing the gap between knowing something is wrong and knowing what to do about it.
  </Accordion>

  </Accordion>
