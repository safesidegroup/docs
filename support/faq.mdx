This page answers common questions about SafeSide workshops, evaluations, CME credits, and program compatibility. Whether you are a host preparing to deliver an InPlace® Workshop, a clinician curious about continuing education credits, or a clinical leader evaluating how SafeSide fits alongside your existing treatment model, you will find clear answers below.

<AccordionGroup>

  <Accordion title="How does a host deliver InPlace® Learning?">
    SafeSide intentionally designed InPlace® Workshops to place a very light burden on the host. You do not need any special knowledge or background in suicide prevention to host a session — you simply need to be comfortable with the tech setup and streaming video.

    During the workshop, your role as host is to:

    - Convey enthusiasm and set a positive tone for the group
    - Advance the videos at the appropriate moments
    - Keep participants on track during discussions and practice exercises

    Everything you need to know about facilitating an InPlace® Workshop fits on a two-page handout called **Getting Started with InPlace® Learning**, which you can find in your SafeSide Account. A 10-minute video overview is also available that walks you through the SafeSide Framework, the InPlace Learning format, and the host role in detail.

    <Tip>
      If you are facilitating a remote group, look for the **Guide to InPlace® Learning with Remote Groups** in your SafeSide Account for additional guidance tailored to virtual delivery.
    </Tip>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is measured in SafeSide Prevention's standard evaluation?">
    When participants consistently complete the pre- and post-evaluation offered as a standard part of your SafeSide program, the data captures several meaningful outcomes:

    - **Workshop completion** — confirming that participants finished the training
    - **Self-efficacy** — participants' confidence in applying what they learned
    - **Learning transfer** — the degree to which participants connect workshop content to their real-world work

    When rated positively, self-efficacy and learning transfer are demonstrated predictors that participants will integrate suicide prevention skills and concepts into their day-to-day work with people at risk. Consistent evaluation completion across your organization gives you reliable data to share with leadership and funders.

    <Note>
      Review the [5-slide overview deck](https://safesideprevention.com/media/documents/SafeSide-Standard-Evaluation-Constructs-2021-12-02.pdf) for additional detail on the standard evaluation constructs.
    </Note>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I complete my post-workshop evaluation?">
    You have two easy ways to access the post-workshop evaluation:

    1. **On-screen prompt** — Towards the end of the final module in the InPlace Workshop, a link to the evaluation appears on-screen, prompting you to complete it before the session closes.
    2. **Participant Workbook** — The link also appears at the end of your Participant Workbook, which the host provides to all participants at the start of the workshop.

    <Tip>
      Complete the evaluation as soon as the prompt appears — it only takes a few minutes and is required if you plan to request CME credits.
    </Tip>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I receive continuing education credits for participating in a SafeSide InPlace Workshop?">
    Yes. Eligible participants can request **3 AMA (American Medical Association) Category 1 CME credits** when submitting their post-workshop evaluation.

    Here is how the process works:

    - CME credits are provided by the **University of Rochester School of Medicine and Dentistry**, accredited by the ACCME to certify continuing medical education for physicians.
    - Select the CME credit option when you submit your post-evaluation.
    - Requests are **processed once a quarter**. Once the University of Rochester makes credits available, you will receive a notification from SafeSide with instructions on how to access your CME transcript.

    **Requesting credit from a different licensing or professional body?** You can use the [SafeSide InPlace Workshop Description and Learning Objectives PDF](https://safesideprevention.com/media/documents/SafeSide-InPlace-Workshop-Description-and-Learning-Objectives.pdf) to support your application to other organizations.

    <Note>
      CME credits are available to physicians and other clinicians who meet eligibility requirements. If you are unsure whether you qualify, check with your licensing body or contact SafeSide.
    </Note>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do you offer training for individuals as well as organizations?">
    SafeSide currently offers training to **organizations only**. This allows the program to be delivered in the context of a team or workforce, which is central to how the SafeSide Framework is designed to work — building shared language and practice across an entire system of care.

    That said, SafeSide is accepting applications to an **early access program for individual clinicians**. If you are a solo practitioner or are interested in accessing SafeSide content independently, reach out to the SafeSide team to learn more about early access availability.

    <Tip>
      If you are exploring SafeSide for your organization, the [contact page](/support/contact) is the fastest way to get a conversation started about program options.
    </Tip>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is treatment with the CAMS model compatible with the SafeSide Framework?">
    Yes — CAMS and the SafeSide Framework are highly compatible and complementary.

    The **Collaborative Assessment and Management of Suicidality (CAMS)** is an evidence-based treatment for suicide ideation developed by Dr. David Jobes. SafeSide and CAMS share a deep philosophical and clinical commitment to a narrative and collaborative process, meaning that staff already trained in CAMS will find themselves on familiar ground with the SafeSide Framework.

    Within the SafeSide Framework, CAMS falls under **"Treatments and Mini-interventions"** — the first category in the **Respond** task. This category refers to offering suicide-specific, evidence-based treatments when possible. The SafeSide InPlace® Workshop explicitly lists CAMS as one of the recommended treatments for addressing drivers of suicide risk.

    Mini-interventions — tactics, phrases, and techniques drawn from manualized evidence-based treatment — can also be applied by staff across a variety of roles, not just clinicians delivering full CAMS protocols.

    <Note>
      If your organization currently provides CAMS treatment, SafeSide can help you define and communicate the complementary fit between CAMS and the SafeSide Framework to your leadership and staff.
    </Note>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How long does it take to get started with SafeSide?">
    The timeline depends on the scope and complexity of your program. Simpler setups — such as a single InPlace® Workshop for a small team — can move quickly from initial contact to delivery. Larger, customized training pathways for health systems or multi-site organizations involve agreed-upon milestones and a more structured onboarding process.

    The best first step is to [schedule a call](/support/contact) with the SafeSide team to discuss your organization's needs and get a realistic timeline estimate for your specific situation.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What does SafeSide cost?">
    Pricing depends on the specific programs you select and the size of your organization. SafeSide takes a **cost-transparent approach**, meaning you will receive a clear picture of what is included and what the investment covers — no opaque pricing or hidden fees.

    Most organizations find that the cost of a SafeSide program is less than the cost of a single lost training day. To get a tailored estimate, [contact the SafeSide team](/support/contact) with your organization name, country, and the programs you are interested in.
  </Accordion>
