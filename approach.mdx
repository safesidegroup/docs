Effective suicide prevention cannot rest on a single training or a single clinician — it requires the entire system to work together. SafeSide Prevention's approach, developed by University of Rochester Professor Tony Pisani in collaboration with leading researchers and practitioners, organizes suicide prevention into three interconnected domains: **Culture**, **Practice**, and **Education and Development**. This framework operationalizes what is commonly called Zero Suicide, adding specificity and substance to guide your organization in selecting, sequencing, and evaluating prevention activities that work together as a whole.

<Note>
  SafeSide is flexible about terminology. Whether or not your organization formally uses the "Zero Suicide" framework or its original keywords (Lead, Train, Identify, Engage, Treat, Transition, Improve), SafeSide's systems approach maps directly onto those goals. You don't need to change your existing language to benefit from this model.
</Note>

## The Three Core Domains

SafeSide's systems model breaks prevention down into three domains that are deeply interconnected and must be developed together. Think of Culture as tilling the soil, Practice as planting the seed, and Education and Development as the water that makes it grow.

## Culture: Building Organizational Foundations

A prevention-oriented culture is not an abstract ideal — it is made up of concrete, measurable dimensions that SafeSide helps you build and sustain.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Connected, Safe, and Resilient Workplace Relationships">
    A healthy, cohesive, well-connected workforce is essential for conveying health and wellness to the people your organization serves. If your team feels disconnected, unsafe, or governed by unhealthy norms, it becomes far harder to extend that care outward.

    SafeSide's **Core Connections** program — originally developed and tested with the US Air Force by Dr. Peter Wyman at the University of Rochester through a randomized controlled trial — builds cohesion, morale, and healthy norms across workplace settings. The program has since been validated in police departments, churches, and youth-serving organizations, demonstrating its adaptability across diverse contexts.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Lived Experience Integrated Into Culture, Practice, and Education">
    Truly integrating lived experience — not just acknowledging it — is critical for effective prevention. SafeSide encourages organizations to ensure that people with lived experience of suicide are involved not only in advisory roles but in leadership, service design, and education development.

    Critically, this means engaging both people who have used your services and those who have not — since avoiding care often signals unmet needs that only community-level outreach can reveal. SafeSide's programs are co-taught, co-led, and co-developed with lived experience faculty, and SafeSide can assist your organization in building structured, funded roles to recognize and sustain these contributions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Restorative, Just, and Learning Culture">
    How your organization responds after a suicide-related incident shapes everything that follows. Administrative reviews that focus on blame and policy violations often compound trauma for clinicians, families, and others affected — rather than promoting healing and improvement.

    SafeSide's **Restore** program, developed in collaboration with Dr. Kathy Turner and colleagues at Queensland Health, equips leaders with a restorative, just, and learning approach to critical incident review. Instead of asking "what went wrong and who's to blame," this approach asks "who has been hurt, and what do they need?" — involving clinicians, families, and organizational leaders in a process that yields better recommendations, greater buy-in, and genuine healing.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Practice: Policies, Pathways, and Continuous Improvement

Culture sets the foundation, but evidence-based practice is where prevention becomes operational.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Best Practices, Policies, and Pathways">
    Your organization's policies and care pathways should promote not just safety, but recovery and health. SafeSide helps you examine how your systems connect with people, assess their concerns, respond with personalized and evidence-based interventions, and extend impact into patients' lives and networks beyond the clinical encounter.

    SafeSide offers policy and practice review alongside program delivery, providing structured guidance on how your procedures can better support best practices — and how those practices can reinforce the cultural and educational elements of your prevention initiative.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Strategies to Explore, Prepare, Implement, and Sustain">
    Implementation planning is not an afterthought — it is the centerpiece of a successful initiative. Drawing on two decades of implementation science, SafeSide helps you develop communication plans, prepare your environment, understand internal and external context variables, and sequence your activities in a way that makes sense for your organization's readiness and priorities.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Data-Driven Continuous Quality Improvement">
    Organizations can be creative in how they measure impact — it doesn't require large-scale, time-intensive data collection to get started. SafeSide works with you to identify a small number of meaningful metrics that can be collected routinely, including behavioral indicators that surface attitudes, skills, and outcomes without relying solely on self-report surveys. These feedback loops allow your organization to adjust as you go, not just at the end of a program cycle.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Education and Development: Building a Learning Workforce

"Training" understates what's needed. SafeSide's approach to workforce education is designed to be sustainable, scalable, and deeply woven into how your organization operates — not a one-time event.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Shared Framework and Common Language">
    When people across teams, services, and sites share a common framework and language, collaboration improves, the experience of care becomes more consistent, and people can move between systems without falling through gaps. SafeSide's **C.A.R.E. Framework** — built around four evidence-based tasks: **Connect, Assess, Respond, and Extend** — provides that shared map. It serves as both an education tool and a co-reflection tool for teams and individuals, giving everyone a clear, practical schema for thinking through complex situations.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="InPlace® Learning: Scalable, Sustainable Education Delivery">
    Developed by Dr. Pisani and his colleagues at the University of Rochester, **InPlace® Learning** is SafeSide's model for education that is both convenient and deeply interactive. It is built around three reinforcing components:

    - **InPlace® Workshop** — A video-guided half-day training event where a local host facilitates group learning through clinical content, real demonstrations, and discussion prompts. No external trainer is needed; any staff member can host, and it can be repeated for new staff at any time.
    - **SafeSide Live Learning Sessions** — Monthly Q&A sessions and expert-led deep dives (called In Focus sessions) open to all subscribers. SafeSide faculty — clinicians and people with lived experience — run these regularly so staff can ask questions, share challenges, and stay current with evolving best practices.
    - **Tools and Refreshers** — Short self-paced modules released throughout the year to onboard new staff, revisit drifted skills, and respond to updated clinical guidance.

    This approach combines the fidelity and repeatability of online content with the collaborative depth of group learning, making it scalable to every new hire and sustainable across your whole workforce over time.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Flexibility for Adaptation of Content and Process">
    Education should be adaptable to the local setting, population, and initiative. SafeSide's programs are designed with this flexibility from the ground up — ranging from natural customization that emerges when groups discuss how best practices apply to their work, to intensive co-development partnerships with organizations and governments.

    One example: SafeSide worked with a Primary Health Network and a suicide prevention collaborative in the Illawarra Shoalhaven region of New South Wales, Australia to adapt its core program for the Alcohol and Other Drugs sector — integrating local lived experience and tailoring content to that community's specific needs and culture.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Real-World Partners

SafeSide's approach is not theoretical — it is grounded in active partnerships with organizations across the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and the United Kingdom.

- **University of Rochester Recovery Center of Excellence** — In collaboration with the UR Medicine Recovery Center of Excellence, funded by the Health Resources & Services Administration, SafeSide developed a no-cost eLearning resource and podcast supporting friends and family of individuals with substance use concerns and suicide risk. Through the same partnership, SafeSide has reached thousands of staff working with at-risk people in rural communities across the US.

- **State of Utah Department of Health and Human Services** — SafeSide has worked with Utah DHS to deliver prevention programs to hundreds of staff supporting youth and families statewide, including an annual full-day youth suicide prevention workshop and a system-wide rollout to all staff within Utah Juvenile Justice and Youth Services.

- **Lexington Health** — What began as a small pilot in a few South Carolina practices grew into a full health system implementation spanning over **60 locations** and reaching more than **1,000 doctors, nurses, and healthcare professionals** — demonstrating what sustained, system-wide prevention looks like in practice.
