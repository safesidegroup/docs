The SafeSide Framework is a map of evidence-based and best practices in suicide prevention. It is designed to be versatile and strategic — useful at every level of an organization, from the individual staff member navigating a difficult conversation to the systems leader evaluating service-wide policy. As SafeSide's guiding principle puts it: _What you do matters. How you do it matters more._

The framework organizes suicide prevention into four interconnected elements: **Connect**, **Assess**, **Respond**, and **Extend (CARE)**. Together, they give your workforce a shared language, a structured approach, and a clear path forward — regardless of role or setting.

## The Four Elements of CARE

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Connect" icon="handshake">
    Connect is the foundation of the framework. It involves asking a person directly about suicide while taking time to understand their individual context — their history, circumstances, relationships, and what matters most to them. Connect means collaborating with the person and their support network rather than acting unilaterally, and it means committing to their safety and well-being over time — not just in the moment of crisis.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Assess" icon="clipboard-list">
    Assess uses a structured approach to gather, organize, and share information to build a clearer picture of the person's situation. That information is then used to articulate a **prevention-oriented risk formulation** — one that moves the team toward person-specific plans and supports rather than generic responses.

    Prevention-oriented risk formulation is a central pillar of the Suicide Prevention Pathway implemented by Gold Coast Health in Queensland, Australia. Recent evidence from that implementation shows a **35% reduction in repeated suicide attempts** for those who received care on the Pathway.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Respond" icon="circle-check">
    Respond covers the range of evidence-based strategies that directly target suicidal drivers. This includes evidence-based treatments, mini-interventions, contingency and safety planning, means safety, appropriate observation, and referrals for unmet needs. The Respond element ensures that assessment leads to purposeful, specific action — not just documentation.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Extend" icon="arrows-turn-right">
    Extend provides skills that support a person in their life — not just during a clinical encounter. It includes non-demand caring contacts, collaborative shared plans with clear roles for family members, carers, and other supports, structured follow-up assessments and connections to crisis services, warm handovers between providers, and a consistent approach across systems and communities. Extend is what transforms a single interaction into sustained care.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## How Different Roles Use the Framework

The SafeSide Framework is not just for clinicians. It is designed to be useful at every level of an organization — from frontline staff to system leaders.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Individual Staff">
    For staff working directly with people experiencing suicidal thoughts or crisis, the framework provides a guide for each interaction and a way to conceptualize the full picture of what best-practice care looks like. It supports consistent, confident decision-making across the range of situations staff encounter in practice.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Supervisors and Teams">
    Supervisors and teams use the framework as a structure for efficient case reviews, co-reflection, and collaborative problem-solving. Rather than relying on ad hoc conversation or individual judgment alone, teams can use the four elements to systematically think through where a person is, what has been done, and what still needs to happen.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Quality Improvement and Systems Leaders">
    For those evaluating and improving services at a systems level, the framework provides a lens for reviewing policy, service design, and care pathways. Leaders can use it to identify where current practice falls short of best practice — and to plan targeted improvements to close the gap.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Learning and Development Leaders">
    Learning and development leaders use the framework to identify focus areas for suicide prevention education across their workforce. The four elements make it straightforward to map existing training against best practice, prioritize gaps, and build a coherent education plan that aligns with organizational goals.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
