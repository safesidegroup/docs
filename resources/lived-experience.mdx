Lived experience is not an add-on at SafeSide — it is foundational. People who have experienced suicidal thoughts, survived a suicide attempt, supported a loved one through crisis, or been bereaved by suicide are involved at every stage of SafeSide's work: shaping content before it is ever filmed, informing how programs are implemented in real organizations, and appearing on screen alongside clinical experts so that learning reflects the full human reality of suicide prevention. The goal is care that is not just evidence-based, but genuinely compassionate and grounded in what it actually feels like to be on the receiving end of it.

## How Lived Experience Is Embedded in SafeSide

  <Card title="Co-Designing Content and Resources" icon="pen-to-square">

  <Card title="Informing Implementation and Partnerships" icon="building-user">

  <Card title="Storytelling Across Programs" icon="microphone">

Contributors share their experiences on screen, offering staff an honest view of what it is like to seek help, be asked about suicide, or support someone through crisis. This is not tokenistic representation — it is central to how SafeSide teaches.

## Why It Matters: Voices from Lived Experience Contributors

The following quotes come directly from lived experience contributors whose perspectives have shaped SafeSide's programs.

> *"If I had to impart one message to services … it would be that you don't have to take responsibility for my success or failure. You just have to sit with me and we can work through it together."*
>
> — **Jay Gardener**, lived experience contributor to SafeSide CARE Alcohol and Other Drugs

---

> *"She had a conversation with me and said, 'Look, this is a journey, but what I can tell you is, things will improve if we work on them.' She didn't make any promises; she didn't say, 'Look, give me two weeks, and you'll be fine' or 'Give me a month, and you'll be fine.' But she gave me a sense of hope."*
>
> — **Sam Fewings**, Lived Experience Faculty

---

> *"I'd started to engage. I went and knocked on lots of doors, and I found that, you know, some services were happy to work with me and around drugs. Some people were happy to work with me around talking about the sexual assault. But everyone wanted to put me in a bucket or only wanted to work on one thing."*
>
> — **Neil Fraser**, lived experience contributor to SafeSide CARE Alcohol and Other Drugs

>
> — **Kristina Zurich**, Lived Experience Faculty and Instructor

## How Lived Experience Shows Up in SafeSide Programs

Lived experience does not appear in SafeSide programs as a brief segment or a single narrator. It is woven throughout:

- **Co-facilitation on screen** — SafeSide CARE workshops are co-led by clinical and lived experience expertise. Staff hear from both — not as separate tracks, but as a genuinely integrated conversation.
- **Content co-design** — program scenarios, discussion prompts, and practice tools are developed with lived experience input, so they reflect real dynamics rather than clinical abstractions.
- **Cultural adaptation** — when programs are customized for specific communities or sectors, lived experience contributors from those communities are central to the process, helping ensure content resonates and avoids harm.

<Note>
  All SafeSide educational programs are co-led by clinical and lived experience expertise. </Note>
